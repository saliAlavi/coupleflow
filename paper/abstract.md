**Title:** CoupleFlow: Null-Calibrated Auditory Attention Decoding With Time-Locked Brain–Speech Coupling and Flow-Matching Likelihood Ratios

**Abstract:**

The auditory cortex can follow the talker a listener attends to when several people talk at once. Auditory attention decoding (AAD) asks whether that talker can be identified from a few seconds of non-invasive brain recording, by matching the recording against the speech of each candidate talker, where neuro-steered hearing devices are a potential long-term application. Recent decoders report near-perfect accuracy from a second of electroencephalography (EEG). We show that these results do not generalize to unheard speech, because the decoders exploit leaked trial information, eye gaze and acoustic cues rather than the neural response to speech. We introduce CoupleFlow, a decoder that reaches a decision through the time-locked coupling between a neural embedding and each candidate talker's acoustic embedding. It is therefore at chance whenever the brain signal carries no meaningful information, so no acoustic or identity shortcut can inflate its accuracy in multimodal setting. A conditional flow-matching head adds a likelihood-ratio decision rule, a conditional flow-matching head adds a likelihood-ratio decision rule: it selects the talker with the highest pointwise mutual information between speech and brain, which is Bayes-optimal when the recording depends only on the attended talker and removes any preference for speech model finds likely a priori. We pair the model with a leakage-free protocol: content-disjoint splits recovered from the audio itself and null tests that separate true time-locking from trial recognition. CoupleFlow outperforms linear and deep baselines trained on identical splits, while every null stays at chance across nine EEG and MEG datasets under one preprocessing pipeline, covering four languages, high-density caps, hearing-impaired listeners and a multi-talker room. Finally, we derive an evidence-accumulation law that predicts accuracy at any window length from a single parameter per dataset, and predicts that parameter from how strongly the brain tracks speech. The law shows that fast, reliable decoding is limited by the signal-to-noise ratio of the recording, and gives the window any target accuracy requires.
